# Claim Ledger

Mortartium uses a claim ledger to prevent material evidence, symbolic interpretation, and speculative inference from collapsing into one another.

Every substantive claim should be entered here or traceable to an artifact card that uses the same grading discipline.

## Claim grades

| Grade | Meaning | Required support |
|---|---|---|
| Established | Ordinary documented material, historical, conservation, or craft fact. | Reliable source or direct object evidence. |
| Strongly supported | Supported by multiple independent evidence types. | At least two distinct source types, e.g. conservation report plus historical record. |
| Plausible | Bounded inference supported by partial evidence, analogy, or an established interpretive tradition. | Clear evidence base plus explicit limits. |
| Speculative | Symbolically coherent or research-generating but not materially proven. | Must be labeled as conjectural. |
| Open test | A concrete unresolved question with a defined evidence path. | Specific evidence needed. |
| Rejected | Contradicted, overbroad, unsupported, or not responsibly maintainable. | Reason for rejection. |

## Ledger fields

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Claim ID | Stable identifier, e.g. `MORT-CLAIM-0001`. |
| Claim | The exact claim in one sentence. |
| Grade | Established, strongly supported, plausible, speculative, open test, or rejected. |
| Layer | Material, process, artifact, symbolic, institutional, restoration, or comparative. |
| Evidence | Sources, artifact cards, conservation data, field notes, or textual references. |
| Limits | What the claim does not establish. |
| Open tests | What would upgrade, downgrade, or reject the claim. |
| Status | Active, superseded, rejected, or parked. |

## Initial claims

### MORT-CLAIM-0001 — Destructive transformation is common in art-material production

**Claim:** Many art and sacred-craft materials are produced through destructive transformation, including burning, firing, grinding, dissolving, scraping, stretching, cutting, binding, and polishing.

**Grade:** Established.

**Layer:** Process.

**Evidence:** To be populated from artifact cards for lime mortar, brick, glass, parchment, pigment, bone black, hide glue, ceramics, metalwork, and instruments.

**Limits:** This does not imply hidden ritual use, human remains, or intentional mystery-school doctrine in every case.

**Open tests:** Add representative source-backed cards across at least five material classes.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-CLAIM-0002 — Mortar is a sacrificial binder in historic masonry

**Claim:** In historic masonry, mortar functions as a joint and binder that can protect the masonry units by acting as a sacrificial or repairable medium.

**Grade:** Established pending source card completion.

**Layer:** Material / restoration.

**Evidence:** Historic repointing and lime mortar sources; artifact card pending.

**Limits:** The symbolic phrase “death-bind” is interpretive, not strict etymology.

**Open tests:** Add restoration-source quotations and mortar chemistry notes.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-CLAIM-0003 — “Death-bind” is a symbolic interpretation, not strict etymology

**Claim:** Mortar is not etymologically derived from Latin `mors/mortis`, but it can be interpreted symbolically as a death-bind because it recomposes burned, broken, mineral, and inert matter into architectural body.

**Grade:** Plausible.

**Layer:** Symbolic / philological.

**Evidence:** Etymology of `mortarium`; material process of mortar production; masonry symbolism.

**Limits:** This must not be presented as dictionary etymology.

**Open tests:** Add glossary entry for `mortarium`, `mort-`, mortar, mortification, and death-bind.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-CLAIM-0004 — Sacred architecture often organizes space around remains

**Claim:** Many sacred architectural systems organize space around bodily remains, relics, tombs, crypts, ossuaries, or contact relics.

**Grade:** Established pending source cards.

**Layer:** Artifact / institutional.

**Evidence:** Relic architecture, altar relics, ossuaries, catacombs, reliquaries; source cards pending.

**Limits:** This does not prove that remains were used in every construction material or stained-glass colorant.

**Open tests:** Add site cards for relic altar, ossuary, crypt, and reliquary.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-CLAIM-0005 — Stained-glass organ/color claims are hard claims

**Claim:** Claims that specific human organs or remains were used to produce specific cathedral stained-glass colors are hard claims requiring object-specific material or archival evidence.

**Grade:** Open test.

**Layer:** Hard claim / material.

**Evidence:** Not yet established.

**Limits:** Symbolic organ-color correspondences do not prove material organ use.

**Open tests:** Search conservation reports, recipe books, pigment/glass analyses, relic inventories, and workshop manuals for bone ash, phosphate, apatite, blood, collagen, animal glue, organ matter, or relic dust.

**Status:** Active.
