# Hard Claims Protocol

Mortartium permits difficult claims, but only as controlled claims. A hard claim is a claim that would materially change the interpretation of an artifact, site, institution, or tradition if true, and that cannot be established by symbolic coherence alone.

Hard claims are not forbidden. They are staged as tests.

## Definition

A hard claim is any claim involving one or more of the following:

- Human remains in material media.
- Human organs, blood, bone, ash, cremains, relic dust, hair, skin, or tissue used in art, architecture, glass, pigment, mortar, plaster, or ritual media.
- Secret, initiatic, Masonic, Rosicrucian, alchemical, or mystery-school intentionality behind a specific object or site.
- Direct continuity between ancient mystery schools and later institutions.
- Claims that symbolic correspondence determines physical recipe.
- Claims that conservation chemistry reveals hidden ritual use.
- Claims that a restoration layer preserves original intention rather than later intervention.

## Required distinction

Hard claims must separate:

1. **Material claim** — what the artifact is physically made from.
2. **Process claim** — how the material was transformed.
3. **Symbolic claim** — what the artifact means or encodes.
4. **Institutional claim** — who authorized, controlled, or interpreted the artifact.
5. **Continuity claim** — how the practice or symbol moved across time.
6. **Reception claim** — how later interpreters read the artifact.

A claim may be true at one layer and unproven at another.

## Evidence ladder

| Level | Evidence type | Use |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | Symbolic analogy only | May generate hypothesis; cannot establish material claim. |
| L1 | Esoteric or interpretive text | Supports symbolic/reception history; not sufficient for composition. |
| L2 | Historical recipe or workshop record | Supports possible practice; must be tied to object class or context. |
| L3 | Object-specific archival record | Strong support for institutional/process claim. |
| L4 | Object-specific conservation analysis | Strong support for material claim. |
| L5 | Multi-method confirmation | Strongest support: chemistry + provenance + contamination control + archive or context. |

## Initial hard claims

### MORT-HARD-0001 — Organ-derived cathedral colorants

**Claim under test:** Specific human or animal organs were used to produce, charge, stain, or symbolically determine specific cathedral stained-glass colors.

**Current grade:** Open test.

**Evidence present:** Symbolic organ-color-humor-planet correspondences are historically plausible in esoteric and medical systems; cathedral glass is materially and symbolically layered.

**Evidence missing:** Object-specific glass or paint chemistry, recipes, workshop records, conservation reports, or archival records linking organ-derived material to a defined glass object, colorant, stain, paint layer, or restoration material.

**Non-claim boundary:** Do not assert this as fact. Do not infer material organ use from symbolic correspondence.

**Upgrade conditions:** Object-specific evidence identifying organ-derived residue, blood, collagen, proteinaceous material, bone ash, phosphate, relic dust, or an archival recipe tied to a specific object class.

**Status:** Active open test.

### MORT-HARD-0002 — Human remains in mortar or plaster

**Claim under test:** Human remains, cremains, relic dust, bone ash, or body-derived substances were incorporated into specific mortar, plaster, limewash, pigment, or architectural media.

**Current grade:** Open test.

**Evidence present:** Relic-centered architecture is historically established; bone ash and organic binders exist as material classes; symbolic death-bind reading is coherent.

**Evidence missing:** Object-specific material analysis or archival records showing human-derived material in a defined construction medium.

**Non-claim boundary:** Relics in an altar, crypt, or reliquary do not prove remains in mortar or plaster.

**Upgrade conditions:** Lab analysis with contamination controls plus provenance or documentary support.

**Status:** Active open test.

### MORT-HARD-0003 — Esoteric intentionality in a specific masonry artifact

**Claim under test:** A specific masonry artifact encodes Masonic, Rosicrucian, alchemical, or mystery-school doctrine by intentional design.

**Current grade:** Open test.

**Evidence present:** Operative and speculative masonry share vocabulary; many symbolic systems use architecture, stone, ashlar, death, rebuilding, and initiation motifs.

**Evidence missing:** Object-specific iconography, inscription, builder record, patron record, lodge record, ritual correspondence, or documented reception chain.

**Non-claim boundary:** Craft similarity does not prove initiatic intention.

**Upgrade conditions:** Convergent object, textual, institutional, and historical evidence.

**Status:** Active open test.

### MORT-HARD-0004 — Universal royal-art continuity

**Claim under test:** A single continuous royal-art lineage links ancient temple craft, mystery schools, masonry, alchemy, Rosicrucianism, Theosophy, and modern restoration science.

**Current grade:** Speculative / bounded comparative hypothesis.

**Evidence present:** Structural recurrence: death, purification, binding, illumination, inscription, initiation, relics, and restoration appear across many traditions.

**Evidence missing:** Transmission evidence sufficient to establish institutional or doctrinal continuity.

**Non-claim boundary:** Structural recurrence does not prove a single hidden school.

**Upgrade conditions:** Traceable texts, translations, lineages, workshop records, institutional documents, or explicit reception chains.

**Status:** Active but bounded.
