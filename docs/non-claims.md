# Non-Claim Ledger

Mortartium uses an explicit non-claim ledger to prevent symbolic, speculative, or research-generating ideas from being accidentally promoted into material, historical, or scientific claims.

This file is as important as the claim ledger. It records what the project is **not** claiming, what remains outside current evidence, and what must not be inferred from adjacent evidence.

## Rule

A non-claim is not a denial that something is possible. It is a boundary: the repository does not assert the claim unless and until object-specific evidence satisfies the required proof standard.

## Non-claim fields

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Non-Claim ID | Stable identifier, e.g. `MORT-NONCLAIM-0001`. |
| Non-Claim | The statement the project does not assert. |
| Reason | Why this boundary is necessary. |
| Allowed adjacent claims | What may still be said responsibly. |
| Evidence that could reopen | What evidence would convert this from non-claim to open test or claim. |
| Status | Active, superseded, or retired. |

## Active non-claims

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0001 — No general claim that human remains were used in cathedral stained glass

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not claim that medieval or Catholic cathedral stained-glass windows were generally made with human remains, human organs, or human-derived colorants.

**Reason:** Symbolic organ-color correspondence, relic architecture, and sacred color doctrine do not prove material use in glass recipes.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** Mortartium may study stained glass as a light-body artifact, material-transformation technology, sacred-color system, and symbolic aperture. It may also investigate object-specific evidence for bone ash, phosphate, organic residues, or relic-related additions where such evidence exists.

**Evidence that could reopen:** Object-specific conservation chemistry, workshop recipe, archival record, relic inventory, or material analysis identifying human-derived material in a defined glass, paint, stain, mortar, plaster, or restoration layer.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0002 — No inference from phosphate or calcium alone to human remains

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not infer human remains merely from the presence of calcium, phosphate, apatite, lime, ash, carbonate, or bone-like mineral signatures.

**Reason:** These signatures can arise from many ordinary sources, including limestone, shell, animal bone, mineral pigments, contamination, restoration materials, soil, mortar, plaster, and environmental processes.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** Mortartium may treat phosphate or calcium signatures as prompts for further material analysis where provenance and context justify it.

**Evidence that could reopen:** Confirmatory multi-method analysis, provenance control, contamination exclusion, species-level or human-specific identification, isotopic evidence, or archival corroboration.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0003 — No claim that symbolic correspondence proves material recipe

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not claim that symbolic correspondences among organs, planets, humors, metals, colors, and saints prove the physical use of corresponding substances in art materials.

**Reason:** Symbolic systems can organize meaning without determining workshop material practice.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** Mortartium may study correspondences as interpretive, initiatic, medical, alchemical, or iconographic systems.

**Evidence that could reopen:** A recipe, lab result, object file, or workshop record linking the symbolic correspondence to a specific material preparation.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0004 — No claim that all masonry is esoteric masonry

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not claim that every masonry artifact, mortar joint, brick wall, repair, or architectural feature encodes intentional Masonic, Rosicrucian, alchemical, or mystery-school doctrine.

**Reason:** Operative craft processes can be materially significant without intentional initiatic coding.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** Mortartium may study how operative craft vocabularies become symbolic, ritual, or speculative doctrines in particular institutions and texts.

**Evidence that could reopen:** Object-specific iconography, inscription, patronage record, lodge record, ritual text, builder record, or documented initiatic context.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0005 — No strict etymological claim that mortar means death-bind

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not claim that the English word `mortar` is etymologically derived from Latin `mors/mortis` meaning death.

**Reason:** The standard etymological line runs through Latin `mortarium`, a vessel or trough for pounding or mixing.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** Mortartium may use `death-bind` as a symbolic or constructed etymology because mortar materially recomposes burned, broken, inert, mineral, and sometimes organic matter into architectural body.

**Evidence that could reopen:** Not applicable for strict etymology unless new philological evidence appears. The symbolic reading remains allowed only as symbolic interpretation.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0006 — No claim that relic-centered architecture proves remains in construction media

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not claim that because sacred architecture often contains relics, those remains were therefore mixed into mortar, plaster, pigment, glass, or structural media.

**Reason:** Relic presence and construction material composition are distinct claims.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** Mortartium may study relics as architectural centers, institutional anchors, devotional technologies, and sacred-presence objects.

**Evidence that could reopen:** Object-specific material analysis or archival evidence showing relic matter or remains were incorporated into a defined construction medium.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0007 — No universal mystery-school continuity claim

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not claim that all destructive transformation practices across Egypt, Greece, India, China, Christianity, alchemy, Freemasonry, Rosicrucianism, Theosophy, Anthroposophy, and modern restoration science derive from one continuous hidden school.

**Reason:** Cross-cultural analogy, structural recurrence, and later syncretic mapping do not prove institutional continuity.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** Mortartium may compare recurring structures: death, purification, binding, illumination, inscription, initiation, relics, and restoration.

**Evidence that could reopen:** Specific transmission evidence: texts, lineages, workshop records, migration paths, institutional documents, translations, borrowings, or traceable reception histories.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0008 — No claim that restoration is neutral

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not claim that restoration records or conservation interventions are neutral reproductions of original artifact states.

**Reason:** Restoration can preserve, alter, erase, add, stabilize, contaminate, reinterpret, or document an object.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** Mortartium treats restoration as evidence-bearing but layered: original fabric, decay, repair, reconstruction, documentation, and interpretive choice must be separated.

**Evidence that could reopen:** Not applicable. This is an enduring methodological boundary.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0009 — No claim that contemporary memorial uses prove medieval practice

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not claim that modern cremains art, memorial glass, memorial diamonds, bone china, or funerary ceramics prove equivalent medieval or ancient practices.

**Reason:** Modern examples prove material possibility, not historical continuity.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** Mortartium may use modern examples to demonstrate material feasibility and contemporary persistence of remains-to-art transformation.

**Evidence that could reopen:** Historical recipes, object studies, archaeology, or archival sources showing comparable practices in a defined earlier context.

**Status:** Active.

### MORT-NONCLAIM-0010 — No claim that Hall, Steiner, or esoteric authors are material-science authorities

**Non-Claim:** Mortartium does not treat Manly P. Hall, Rudolf Steiner, Theosophical, Rosicrucian, alchemical, or Masonic texts as sufficient material-science evidence for object composition.

**Reason:** Esoteric texts can support symbolic interpretation and intellectual history, but they do not by themselves establish chemical composition or workshop practice.

**Allowed adjacent claims:** These sources may be used for symbolic grammar, reception history, initiatic vocabulary, and comparative interpretation.

**Evidence that could reopen:** Independent material, archival, or conservation evidence that confirms a specific material claim also found in an esoteric source.

**Status:** Active.
