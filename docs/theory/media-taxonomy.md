# Media Taxonomy

The media taxonomy answers one question: what kinds of matter can become carriers of art, memory, ritual, architecture, or authority?

This taxonomy is not a list of claims. It is a field map. Each medium must later be connected to examples, sources, rites, forms, symbolic operations, and claim boundaries.

## How to read a medium

For each medium, ask:

1. What is the source matter?
2. What remains or residue does it involve?
3. What destructive transformation produces the medium?
4. What art or architectural techniques use it?
5. What forms does it become?
6. What symbolic operations can it support?
7. What evidence would teach it responsibly?
8. What must not be inferred from it?

## 1. Human bodily media

### Bone

**Source matter:** skeleton, skull, tooth, fragment, relic.

**Transformations:** preservation, cutting, polishing, carving, burning to ash, grinding, display, containment.

**Media / techniques:** relics, ossuaries, reliquaries, bone carving, bone ash, pigment hypothesis, memorial art, archaeological specimen.

**Forms:** skull wall, bone chapel, reliquary, tomb deposit, amulet, memorial object, museum object.

**Symbolic operations:** relic-core, memento mori, ancestor transmission, death-as-architecture, bone-to-color, containment.

**Examples to source later:** Sedlec Ossuary, Capuchin Crypt, catacomb saints, reliquary bones.

**Boundary:** bone presence in one context does not prove bone in mortar, glass, pigment, or brick.

### Ash / cremains

**Source matter:** cremated human or animal remains; mineral residue after burning.

**Transformations:** cremation, collection, sealing, mixing, scattering, incorporation into memorial media.

**Media / techniques:** urn, cinerary altar, columbarium, memorial glass, memorial ceramic, ash deposit, possible ritual media.

**Forms:** urn, altar cavity, tomb base, column base, memorial jewel, ceramic vessel, sealed reliquary.

**Symbolic operations:** royal furnace, containment, resurrection, grave-as-axis, body reduced to essence.

**Examples to source later:** Trajan ash-base tradition, Roman cinerary altars, modern memorial glass/ceramic.

**Boundary:** ash-like material can come from many sources. Human cremains require object-specific evidence.

### Blood

**Source matter:** human or animal blood.

**Transformations:** drying, staining, coagulation, mixing, ritual offering, symbolic representation.

**Media / techniques:** ritual mark, paint/binder hypothesis, sacrifice narrative, relic blood, martyr cult, blood-earth motif.

**Forms:** blood relic, stain, ritual mark, literary motif, possible binder or pigment claim under test.

**Symbolic operations:** sacrifice, blood-earth, covenant, martyrdom, life-force, horror, pollution/purification.

**Examples to source later:** blood relics, martyr cults, literary blood-architecture motifs.

**Boundary:** red color does not prove blood. Symbolic blood does not prove material blood.

### Skin

**Source matter:** human or animal skin. Human skin claims require heightened source discipline.

**Transformations:** flaying, scraping, liming, stretching, tanning, drying, inscription, preservation.

**Media / techniques:** parchment, vellum, leather, tattoo, binding, drumhead, shroud, relic skin.

**Forms:** manuscript, codex, scroll, drum, garment, bookbinding, relic, anthropodermic claim under test.

**Symbolic operations:** inscribed skin, flesh becomes text, body as carrier, law/memory on prepared surface.

**Examples to source later:** parchment manuscript, vellum codex, drum skin, shroud, rare anthropodermic bindings.

**Boundary:** animal parchment is ordinary; human skin claims must be object-specific and chemically/historically supported.

### Hair, teeth, organs

**Source matter:** hair, tooth, organ, tissue.

**Transformations:** preservation, enclosure, weaving, reliquary display, medical/esoteric correspondence, possible ritual use.

**Media / techniques:** mourning jewelry, relic, anatomical specimen, symbolic medicine, organ-color correspondence.

**Forms:** reliquary, locket, hairwork, tooth relic, anatomical diagram, symbolic color system.

**Symbolic operations:** identity trace, vitality, organ-world correspondence, humoral color, saintly presence.

**Examples to source later:** hairwork mourning jewelry, tooth relics, organ-color correspondences in esoteric anatomy.

**Boundary:** organ-color symbolic systems do not prove organ-derived material recipes.

## 2. Animal bodily media

### Hide, parchment, vellum, leather

**Source matter:** animal skin.

**Transformations:** liming, scraping, stretching, drying, tanning, polishing, cutting.

**Media / techniques:** manuscript, codex, charter, leather binding, drumhead, shield, garment.

**Forms:** book, scroll, legal document, sacred text, drum, robe, reliquary covering.

**Symbolic operations:** inscribed skin, body as text, transmission, authority, ritual covering.

**Evidence:** codicology, manuscript conservation, material analysis, craft histories.

**Boundary:** ordinary animal skin does not imply hidden ritual use.

### Bone, ivory, horn, shell, tooth

**Source matter:** animal skeletal or hard tissues.

**Transformations:** cutting, carving, polishing, burning, grinding, inlay, engraving.

**Media / techniques:** carving, inlay, pigment, instrument, ornament, comb, handle, amulet.

**Forms:** ivory panel, bone flute, horn vessel, shell inlay, tooth pendant, bone black pigment.

**Symbolic operations:** bone-to-color, sounding remains, ancestor/animal power, luxury, relic-like presence.

**Evidence:** museum catalog, conservation study, species identification, craft history.

**Boundary:** animal bone/ivory evidence does not establish human remains.

### Glue, gelatin, egg, casein, gut

**Source matter:** animal collagen, hide, bone, milk protein, egg, intestine.

**Transformations:** boiling, rendering, drying, stretching, emulsifying, binding.

**Media / techniques:** paint binder, gilding, manuscript illumination, instrument string, adhesive, ground layer.

**Forms:** paint film, gilded surface, instrument string, glued panel, manuscript decoration.

**Symbolic operations:** invisible animal body binding image/sound; connective tissue becomes art support.

**Evidence:** conservation chemistry, FTIR, protein analysis, recipe books.

**Boundary:** proteinaceous binder does not imply human remains or ritual sacrifice.

## 3. Plant media

### Wood and charcoal

**Source matter:** tree, branch, root, timber.

**Transformations:** cutting, drying, carving, burning, charring, polishing, hollowing.

**Media / techniques:** sculpture, architecture, charcoal drawing, instrument body, coffin, panel painting, reliquary.

**Forms:** beam, coffin, panel, statue, charcoal mark, flute, mask, shrine.

**Symbolic operations:** living tree to dead support, world-tree, coffin-body, sounding hollow, carbon mark.

**Evidence:** dendrochronology, conservation, craft history, archaeology.

**Boundary:** wood symbolism does not prove ritual tree cult unless sourced.

### Fiber, textile, paper, papyrus

**Source matter:** flax, hemp, cotton, bark, papyrus, rag, cellulose.

**Transformations:** retting, beating, pulping, spinning, weaving, dyeing, sizing, writing.

**Media / techniques:** textile, shroud, manuscript paper, banner, robe, wrapping, burial cloth.

**Forms:** shroud, veil, book, scroll, garment, flag, relic cloth, mummy wrapping.

**Symbolic operations:** covering, veiling, wrapping, transmission, body extension, social skin.

**Evidence:** textile analysis, fiber ID, manuscript conservation, dye analysis.

**Boundary:** ritual garment meaning must be sourced, not assumed from textile alone.

### Resin, gum, incense, dye plants

**Source matter:** sap, plant exudate, bark, root, flower, insect/plant dye complexes.

**Transformations:** extraction, boiling, fermentation, drying, burning, mordanting.

**Media / techniques:** incense, paint binder, varnish, dye, embalming resin, ritual smoke.

**Forms:** scent, coating, color, preserved body, painted surface, dyed textile.

**Symbolic operations:** purification, offering, preservation, invisible medium, color-body.

**Evidence:** chemical analysis, recipe, botanical ID, ritual text.

**Boundary:** scent or dye symbolism does not establish material recipe without evidence.

## 4. Mineral and earth media

### Clay and brick

**Source matter:** clay, sand, silt, temper, mineral inclusions.

**Transformations:** digging, levigating, mixing, molding, drying, firing, vitrification gradients.

**Media / techniques:** brick, terracotta, ceramic, tile, tablet.

**Forms:** wall, road, tomb, kiln, house, prison, temple, inscription tablet.

**Symbolic operations:** royal furnace, earth hardened by fire, blood-earth motif, modular body, labor memory.

**Evidence:** ceramic analysis, brickmaking history, kiln archaeology, color chemistry.

**Boundary:** red brick does not prove blood; yellow brick does not prove absence of violence.

### Lime, mortar, plaster

**Source matter:** limestone, shell, chalk, sand, ash, crushed brick, water, aggregate.

**Transformations:** calcination, slaking, mixing, carbonation, hydraulic set, decay, leaching.

**Media / techniques:** mortar joint, plaster wall, fresco ground, limewash, repair material.

**Forms:** wall, joint, surface, fresco support, restored masonry, tomb plaster.

**Symbolic operations:** death-bind, repairable joint, sacrificial medium, whitewash, wall-body.

**Evidence:** mortar analysis, petrography, conservation reports, repointing guides.

**Boundary:** mortar as death-bind is symbolic interpretation, not strict etymology or proof of esoteric intent.

### Stone, marble, alabaster, granite

**Source matter:** quarried rock.

**Transformations:** quarrying, cutting, carving, polishing, drilling, inscription, relocation.

**Media / techniques:** sculpture, column, tomb, altar, stele, sarcophagus, foundation, pavement.

**Forms:** statue, column, crypt, cippus, altar, tomb slab, capital, inscription, ruin.

**Symbolic operations:** permanence, body-as-architecture, grave-as-axis, public memory, ruin-as-body.

**Evidence:** quarry studies, tool marks, inscriptions, site records, conservation.

**Boundary:** stone monumentality does not imply burial or hidden remains.

### Sand, glass, enamel, glaze

**Source matter:** silica, flux, stabilizer, metal colorants, ash, minerals.

**Transformations:** melting, vitrification, coloring, staining, leading, firing, painting.

**Media / techniques:** stained glass, enamel, ceramic glaze, glass vessel, memorial glass.

**Forms:** window, bead, vessel, reliquary glass, enamel icon, glazed tile.

**Symbolic operations:** light-body, transparency, celestial color, sealed vision, vitrified memory.

**Evidence:** glass chemistry, conservation, workshop recipes, XRF, Raman.

**Boundary:** color symbolism does not prove organic or organ-derived colorant.

### Metal, ore, pigment salts

**Source matter:** ore, copper, iron, lead, tin, gold, silver, mercury compounds, salts.

**Transformations:** mining, smelting, casting, hammering, gilding, oxidation, patination, grinding.

**Media / techniques:** sculpture, bell, reliquary, pigment, stained-glass colorant, jewelry, coin, seal.

**Forms:** bell, statue, vessel, reliquary, colorant, ring, inscription plate.

**Symbolic operations:** royal authority, planetary metal, voice, durability, sacred shine, poison/power.

**Evidence:** metallurgical analysis, pigment analysis, inscriptions, workshop records.

**Boundary:** alchemical correspondence does not prove ritual use in a specific object.

### Salt, sulfate, carbonate, phosphate

**Source matter:** mineral salts, decay products, groundwater, lime, bone minerals, environmental contamination.

**Transformations:** dissolution, migration, crystallization, efflorescence, carbonation, sulfation.

**Media / techniques:** wall surface, mortar decay, pigment/mineral residue, preservation, ritual salt.

**Forms:** bloom, crust, stain, calthemite, spall, powder, residue.

**Symbolic operations:** salt confession, structure revealing hidden chemistry, purification, decay script.

**Evidence:** ion chromatography, salt analysis, conservation mapping, microscopy.

**Boundary:** phosphate/calcium alone does not prove bone or human remains.

## 5. Civilizational remains and ruins

### Spolia and displaced fabric

**Source matter:** reused columns, stones, inscriptions, capitals, tomb fragments, architectural salvage.

**Transformations:** removal, recutting, relocation, reinstallation, reinterpretation.

**Media / techniques:** architecture, civic monument, museum display, restoration, landscape installation.

**Forms:** reused column, relocated Capitol column, embedded inscription, museum fragment, ruin garden.

**Symbolic operations:** ruin-as-body, civic relic, memory transfer, conquest, continuity, resurrection.

**Evidence:** provenance records, restoration history, architectural analysis, site records.

**Boundary:** authenticity as original fabric does not imply original meaning survives unchanged.

### Ruin and restoration surface

**Source matter:** damaged architecture, cracked plaster, broken stone, patched wall, weathered inscription.

**Transformations:** decay, repair, conservation, cleaning, replacement, reconstruction.

**Media / techniques:** restoration, repointing, anastylosis, museum conservation, documentation.

**Forms:** ruin, restored wall, patched joint, stabilized fragment, reconstructed chamber.

**Symbolic operations:** ruin-as-body, resurrection, scar, witness, forensic reading.

**Evidence:** restoration reports, before/after documentation, conservation records, material study.

**Boundary:** restoration is not neutral and must be separated from original fabric.

## Use rule

Media taxonomy does not prove claims. It gives us the map of possible transformations.

Specific claims require source cards, artifact cards, and claim grades.
