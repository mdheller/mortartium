# Column-Tomb

The Column-Tomb archetype studies vertical funerary and memorial forms: columns, pillars, cippi, funerary altars, columned sarcophagi, crypt supports, victory columns, and civic column-remains.

A column can be a structural member, a monument, a marker, a container-base, a body-axis, a memory device, or a political-theological symbol of ascent.

## Core question

How does a vertical architectural form translate a dead body, ash, name, victory, lineage, office, or civic memory into durable public order?

## Material signatures

- Stone, marble, sandstone, granite, porphyry, brick, concrete, metal, lead, bronze, inscription panels.
- Ash urns, sealed cavities, funerary chambers, relic containers, tomb bases.
- Column shafts, capitals, bases, plinths, cippi, altar blocks, sarcophagus registers.
- Removed original fabric later reinstalled as civic ruin or memorial landscape.

## Destructive transformation processes

- Quarrying stone from landscape body.
- Cutting, carving, fluting, polishing, drilling, hollowing, sealing.
- Burning the dead to ash in cremation contexts.
- Translating bodily remains into urn, chamber, base, inscription, or monument.
- Removing and relocating original architectural fabric during restoration or expansion.

## Artifact forms

- Victory column with funerary chamber or ash deposit.
- Funerary pillar or columnar tomb monument.
- Cippus or funerary altar with ash cavity.
- Columned sarcophagus or architectural sarcophagus front.
- Crypt column array connected to unrealized or realized burial program.
- Removed authentic columns reinstalled as ruins or public monuments.

## Symbolic operations

- **Axis** — the dead are translated into a vertical line between earth, civic order, and heaven.
- **Ascent** — column as apotheosis, victory, honor, or resurrection vector.
- **Containment** — body or ash placed in base, chamber, urn, crypt, or memorial cavity.
- **Inscription** — name, office, family, conquest, or lineage fixed to stone.
- **Public memory** — private mortality converted into collective order.
- **Civic relic** — displaced architectural fabric becomes authentic ruin or state memory object.

## Institutional authority

- Emperor, senate, state, military order, family, priesthood, guild, cathedral chapter, cemetery authority, museum, conservator, national park or arboretum authority.

## Evidence types

- Archaeological reports.
- Funerary inscriptions.
- Restoration records.
- Site histories.
- Conservation reports.
- Object catalogs.
- Architectural drawings.
- Museum provenance records.
- Ancient textual references.

## Claim hazards

1. Do not infer that every column contains remains.
2. Do not infer body-sized construction from column height or diameter without specific evidence.
3. Do not collapse funerary columns, cippi, altars, sarcophagi, crypt columns, and victory columns into one material practice.
4. Do not treat civic column symbolism as proof of literal entombment.
5. Do not confuse authentic architectural provenance with hidden funerary contents.

## Initial open tests

- Identify object classes where column, pillar, cippus, altar, or sarcophagus dimensions are explicitly tied to the deceased body or remains.
- Distinguish full-body inhumation, cremation ash containment, relic containment, and pure memorial marker.
- Map Roman and post-Roman column-tomb forms against civic/state column symbolism.
- Add object cards for Trajan's Column, Igel Column, Roman funerary altars/cippi, Capitol Columns, and the U.S. Capitol Crypt.

## Claim grade

The general column-tomb pattern is established as a funerary and memorial object class. Claims about remains inside a specific column, body-sized proportional design, or intentional esoteric coding require object-specific evidence.
