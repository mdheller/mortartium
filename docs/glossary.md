# Glossary

This glossary keeps Mortartium readable while preserving layer discipline.

Entries are short by design. Longer explanations belong in theory documents, source cards, dossiers, or artifact cards.

## Field terms

### Mortartium

A research atlas on how cultures transform death, remains, bodily matter, ruin, and destructive material processes into art, architecture, ritual authority, and transmissible memory.

**Layers:** all.

**Do not confuse with:** a claim that every example has hidden remains or esoteric intent.

**Related:** `docs/theory/field-map.md`.

### Remains

Matter left after life, use, destruction, decay, or historical displacement.

**Layers:** substrate, reception, restoration.

**Use in Mortartium:** includes human remains, animal remains, plant remains, mineral residues, and civilizational ruins.

**Do not confuse with:** human remains only.

### Substrate

The underlying matter being preserved, transformed, or used as a carrier.

**Layers:** substrate, process, medium.

**Examples:** bone, skin, clay, lime, sand, glass, parchment, brick, stone.

### Medium

The art or craft material that carries transformed matter.

**Layers:** medium, process, form.

**Examples:** mortar, brick, stained glass, parchment, pigment, textile, metal, plaster.

### Form

The stable artifact or architectural shape that organizes a medium into cultural function.

**Layers:** form, symbolic operation, institution.

**Examples:** tomb, column, crypt, reliquary, window, manuscript, wall, drum.

### Rite

A structured practice around death, transition, consecration, restoration, or memory.

**Layers:** rite, institution.

**Examples:** burial, cremation, relic translation, altar consecration, foundation deposit, reburial.

### Reception

The later life of an artifact: how it is read, moved, restored, mythologized, displayed, or reinterpreted.

**Layers:** reception, restoration, evidence.

**Do not confuse with:** original intention.

### Motif

A remembered phrase, symbolic pattern, research suspicion, image, analogy, or cross-tradition recurrence worth investigating.

**Layers:** discovery / intake.

**Do not confuse with:** claim or artifact.

**Related:** `docs/motifs/motif-register.md`.

### Archetype

A reusable interpretive pattern that organizes examples across multiple layers.

**Layers:** symbolic operation, form, process.

**Examples:** Death-Bind, Light-Body, Relic-Core, Royal Furnace.

**Do not confuse with:** proof.

### Artifact card

A structured research note for a concrete object, site, object class, or material process with teachable evidence.

**Layers:** all.

**Do not confuse with:** motif entry.

## Process terms

### Calcination

Heating a material strongly so that it chemically decomposes or transforms, as in burning limestone into quicklime or producing ash.

**Layers:** process, medium.

**Do not confuse with:** ordinary symbolic purification unless the text or context supports that interpretation.

### Slaking

The reaction of quicklime with water to produce slaked lime.

**Layers:** process, medium.

**Use in Mortartium:** central to lime mortar and death-bind analysis.

### Carbonation

The process by which lime mortar or plaster reacts with carbon dioxide and hardens back toward a carbonate form.

**Layers:** process, restoration.

### Firing

Heating clay, brick, ceramic, glass paint, or other material in a kiln or furnace to transform it into a more durable body.

**Layers:** process, medium, Royal Furnace.

### Vitrification

Transformation into glass or glass-like material through heat.

**Layers:** process, medium, Light-Body.

### Smelting

Extracting metal from ore by heat and chemical reduction.

**Layers:** process, medium, Royal Furnace.

### Leaching

Movement of soluble material out of a substrate by water.

**Layers:** process, restoration, Weeping Wall.

### Efflorescence

Crystalline salt deposit that appears on a surface after dissolved salts migrate and water evaporates.

**Layers:** process, restoration, Weeping Wall.

### Spalling

Breaking or flaking of a surface, often caused by internal pressure, salts, corrosion, freeze-thaw, or incompatible repair.

**Layers:** restoration, material decay.

### Putrefaction

Organic decomposition. In alchemical and symbolic contexts, it may also name a stage of breakdown before transformation.

**Layers:** process, symbolic operation.

**Do not confuse with:** literal decay in every symbolic use.

### Fermentation

Biological or chemical transformation by microorganisms or enzymes; also used symbolically in some alchemical contexts.

**Layers:** process.

### Inscription

The act of marking a surface with text, image, name, symbol, seal, or scar.

**Layers:** process, form, symbolic operation.

### Binding

Joining fragments or materials into a new body.

**Layers:** process, symbolic operation.

**Examples:** mortar, glue, resin, stitching, oath, covenant.

## Material terms

### Bone

Skeletal material from human or animal bodies.

**Layers:** substrate, medium, Relic-Core, Bone-to-Color.

**Do not confuse with:** generic calcium or phosphate without object-specific evidence.

### Ash / cremains

Residue after burning organic matter; cremains are cremated remains.

**Layers:** substrate, rite, Royal Furnace.

**Do not confuse with:** any ash-like material as human cremains.

### Blood

Human or animal fluid with strong ritual, symbolic, and biological meanings.

**Layers:** substrate, symbolic operation.

**Do not confuse with:** red pigment, red brick, or red stone without material evidence.

### Skin

Human or animal outer tissue; in ordinary craft contexts usually animal hide.

**Layers:** substrate, Inscribed Skin, Sounding Remains.

### Parchment

Prepared animal skin used as a writing surface.

**Layers:** medium, Inscribed Skin.

**Do not confuse with:** human skin unless object-specific evidence supports that claim.

### Vellum

A fine parchment, often associated with calfskin in traditional usage, though terminology varies by context.

**Layers:** medium, Inscribed Skin.

### Lime

Calcium-based material produced from limestone, shell, or related carbonate sources and used in mortar, plaster, limewash, and construction.

**Layers:** substrate, process, medium.

### Mortar

A binding material used between stones, bricks, or masonry units.

**Layers:** medium, Death-Bind, restoration.

**Do not confuse with:** strict derivation from `mors/mortis`; death-bind is symbolic interpretation.

### Clay

Fine earth material that can be shaped and fired into brick, ceramic, tile, or tablet.

**Layers:** substrate, Royal Furnace.

### Brick

A modular building unit commonly made from clay or related material and hardened by drying and/or firing.

**Layers:** medium, form, Royal Furnace.

**Do not confuse with:** blood evidence based on red color.

### Glass

Vitrified material, often silica-based, used in vessels, windows, beads, enamel, and stained glass.

**Layers:** medium, Light-Body, Royal Furnace.

### Pigment

Coloring material used in paint, ink, plaster, manuscript illumination, glaze, or other media.

**Layers:** medium, process.

### Salt

Soluble mineral or chemical compound that may participate in ritual use, preservation, decay, or efflorescence.

**Layers:** substrate, process, Weeping Wall.

### Phosphate

A chemical class relevant to bone minerals, some pigments, fertilizers, soils, and contamination pathways.

**Layers:** substrate, evidence.

**Do not confuse with:** proof of bone or human remains by itself.

### Gypsum

Calcium sulfate mineral used in plaster and also formed in some decay processes.

**Layers:** substrate, medium, restoration.

### Metal

Ore-derived or worked metallic material used in sculpture, reliquaries, bells, jewelry, seals, colorants, and architecture.

**Layers:** medium, Royal Furnace, authority.

### Spolia

Reused architectural or sculptural fragments from earlier contexts.

**Layers:** civilizational remains, Ruin-as-Body, reception.

**Do not confuse with:** unchanged original meaning.

## Form terms

### Tomb

Constructed or marked place for the dead.

**Layers:** form, rite, institution.

### Crypt

Lower or underground chamber, often associated with burial, relics, support, or sacred descent.

**Layers:** form, Threshold / Initiation, Column-Tomb.

**Do not confuse with:** actual burial unless evidence supports it.

### Cippus

A small upright marker or stone post; in Roman contexts often associated with boundaries or funerary marking.

**Layers:** form, Column-Tomb, inscription.

### Column

Vertical architectural support or monument.

**Layers:** form, Column-Tomb, civic memory.

**Do not confuse with:** remains containment by default.

### Altar

Ritual table, platform, or sacred focal object; in some traditions may contain relics or be consecrated.

**Layers:** rite, form, Relic-Core.

### Reliquary

Container or display object for relics.

**Layers:** form, Relic-Core.

### Ossuary

Container, chamber, or structure for bones, usually after primary decomposition or transfer.

**Layers:** form, Relic-Core, Ruin-as-Body.

### Sarcophagus

Container for a body, often made of stone and sometimes richly carved.

**Layers:** form, burial, inscription.

### Rose window

Circular or radial window, often filled with stained glass.

**Layers:** form, Light-Body.

### Fresco

Wall-painting technique involving pigment applied to plaster, especially wet lime plaster in true fresco.

**Layers:** medium, form, wall-skin.

### Manuscript

Handwritten text object, often on parchment, vellum, paper, or papyrus.

**Layers:** form, Inscribed Skin, institution.

### Ruin

Damaged, broken, or surviving remnant of a structure or object.

**Layers:** reception, Ruin-as-Body.

## Symbolic operation terms

### Death-Bind

Symbolic operation in which broken, dead, burned, or inert matter is recomposed into a durable body.

**Examples:** mortar, glue, mosaic, covenant, joint.

### Light-Body

Symbolic operation in which matter becomes a carrier of radiance, sanctity, revelation, or transformed body.

**Examples:** stained glass, halo, aureole, gold ground, illuminated manuscript.

### Relic-Core

Symbolic operation in which remains or contact objects anchor sacred space, authority, or memory.

**Examples:** altar relic, saint body, reliquary, crypt.

### Royal Furnace

Symbolic operation in which controlled fire destroys, purifies, hardens, or transforms matter into durable form.

**Examples:** kiln, forge, glass furnace, lime kiln, cremation.

### Inscribed Skin

Symbolic operation in which skin, surface, or body becomes a carrier of writing, image, law, or memory.

**Examples:** parchment, tattoo, epitaph, manuscript.

### Bone-to-Color

Symbolic/material lane in which skeletal matter becomes pigment, whiteness, blackness, ceramic body, or memorial medium.

### Blood-Earth

Motif in which red earth, brick, clay, sacrifice, labor, or violence are symbolically linked.

**Do not confuse with:** material blood evidence.

### Grave-as-Axis

Symbolic operation in which a burial or memorial form becomes vertical, axial, or civic.

**Examples:** column, obelisk, stele, tower tomb.

### Salt Confession

Symbolic reading of walls or surfaces revealing hidden chemistry through salts, stains, leaching, or efflorescence.

**Do not confuse with:** intentional symbolism.

### Ruin-as-Body

Symbolic operation in which a broken structure or fragment is read as a body with scars, memory, decay, and restoration.

## Evidence terms

### Material evidence

Evidence from object composition, laboratory analysis, conservation science, archaeology, residue analysis, microscopy, or chemistry.

### Textual evidence

Evidence from written sources: recipes, inscriptions, inventories, ritual manuals, building records, letters, or archives.

### Institutional evidence

Evidence from official bodies: church, state, museum, guild, archive, conservation authority, or heritage agency.

### Symbolic evidence

Evidence from iconography, ritual interpretation, myth, esoteric texts, literary texts, or comparative symbolism.

**Do not confuse with:** material evidence.

### Remembered-source prompt

A source remembered by the researcher but not yet bibliographically identified.

**Use in Mortartium:** valid for motif generation and search targeting.

**Do not confuse with:** source-backed proof.

### Open test

A concrete research question that could be upgraded or rejected by specific evidence.

### Claim

A statement the project asserts at a defined grade.

### Non-claim

A boundary statement explaining what the project does not assert.

### Claim grade

The current evidentiary strength of a claim: established, strongly supported, plausible, speculative, open test, rejected, or mixed.

## Use rule

If a term belongs to more than one layer, define which layer is being used in context.

Most errors in this field come from using a term correctly in one layer and then silently promoting it into another.
