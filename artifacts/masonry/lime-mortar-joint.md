## Ladder walkthrough

### 1. Field question

How does broken or transformed mineral matter become architectural body?

### 2. Layer path

- **Rite / practice field:** construction, repair, restoration, conservation.
- **Substrate:** limestone or shell-derived lime, sand, water, and possible additives.
- **Process:** calcination, slaking, mixing, placement, carbonation, later decay.
- **Medium:** lime mortar.
- **Form:** joint between brick or stone.
- **Symbolic operation:** Death-Bind; repairable joint; wall-body.
- **Institution / authority:** mason, builder, conservator, preservation authority.
- **Reception / restoration:** repointing, repair campaigns, efflorescence, spalling, inappropriate replacement mortar.
- **Evidence boundary:** material and conservation claims require conservation sources; symbolic reading remains interpretive.

### 3. Media class

Mineral and earth media.

### 4. Form class

Wall / road / built-surface form.

### 5. Archetype

Death-Bind; Weeping Wall; Ruin-as-Body.

### 6. Concrete example

Historic lime mortar joint.

### 7. Evidence type

Conservation guidance, mortar analysis, lime-cycle chemistry, restoration records, petrography, field observation.

### 8. Claim boundary

Mortartium may read a mortar joint as Death-Bind: broken, burned, slaked, mixed, and recomposed mineral matter holding a wall-body together.

Mortartium does not claim that mortar strictly derives from `mors/mortis`, nor that every mortar joint encodes intentional esoteric doctrine.

### 9. Open test

Promote this scaffold by adding exact source citations for lime mortar composition, lime cycle chemistry, repointing compatibility, and documented conservation cases.
