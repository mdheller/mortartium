## Reading

Roman funerary altars and cippi are key bridge objects for Mortartium because they sit between grave marker, stone container, inscription, family memory, and vertical monument.

The question is not simply whether “columns contained people.” The more precise investigation is:

- Which Roman funerary stones contained cremated remains or ash vessels?
- Which were only grave markers?
- Which used columnar, altar-like, or architectural forms?
- Which were proportioned to body, urn, status, or family memory?
- Which later readers confused or merged these categories?

## Relation to the anonymous source prompt

The anonymous book-source prompt about entombed people in columns remains active. Roman funerary altars and cippi are one of the first places to test it because they may preserve the relevant pattern in a more specific form: not necessarily a full body in a column shaft, but remains, ash, inscription, and vertical stone form combined in funerary practice.

## Claim boundary

Mortartium does not claim that all Roman columns, cippi, or funerary altars contained bodies.

Mortartium does claim that this object class is a legitimate target for testing the Column-Tomb archetype and the remembered column-entombment hypothesis.
